# Kinematic contact, Foundation 0.7.1

**Status:** experimental CPU contact slice, not a general physics engine. `WorldState` owns at most one analytic spherical `KinematicBody` with a stable `EntityId`, centre, radius, desired velocity, last displacement, and last contact. `SetBodyVelocity` is a tick-indexed input; rendering never changes body state. Speed is bounded to 100 world units per simulated second, radius to 0.001–10 world units, and body centres and sweep endpoints to ±1000 per axis. A step that leaves this domain fails atomically.

## Supported solids and sweep

`ContactScene::from_world` reads ordinary uniformly transformed spheres, organism node spheres, and parent-child capsules. Connections use the same `CONNECTION_RADIUS_RATIO` as rendering. Resource-source markers and the body itself are not obstacles. AABBs, general field/CSG solids, and renderer-only primitives are unsupported. In particular, expanding AABB half extents by the body radius would give incorrect sharp corners, so no AABB contact is claimed.

A body sphere of radius *r* moving from *p* by finite displacement *v* is reduced to a point sweep against each static sphere or capsule with radius *R = r + collider_radius*. Parameter *t* is a fraction in `[0,1]`, with centre `p + t v`. For sphere endpoints, solve `|p + t v − c|² = R²`. For a nondegenerate capsule axis, remove the components parallel to its unit axis and solve the corresponding quadratic for the cylindrical side, accepting only roots whose axial projection lies on the segment. Endpoint sphere roots cover both caps. A degenerate capsule is a sphere. The numerically stable quadratic form is used; a slightly negative or barely positive discriminant within `64 × f64::EPSILON × (b² + |4ac|)` is reported as `ContactError::Indeterminate`, not a safe miss. Exact representable tangency is accepted. This is a heuristic uncertainty window, not a certified floating-point proof.

At the start of a sweep, strict overlap reports contact fraction zero, even for zero displacement; the normal is absent at a coincident centre. A body exactly on a boundary moving inward contacts at zero; motion away from that boundary is allowed. Earliest fraction wins. Exact ties use deterministic world insertion order: ordinary spheres, then organism nodes and connections, each in allocation order. The conservative CPU BVH only rejects swept AABBs disjoint from collider AABBs; direct traversal is the reference. The current world caps bound the scene to 892 solid primitives (512 ordinary spheres plus four organisms with 48 nodes and 47 connections each). Runtime and native viewport retain a disposable contact scene. Each body step gathers the authoritative analytic colliders in stable order and compares IDs, endpoints, radii, and bounds exactly; unchanged geometry reuses the BVH, while any growth, pruning, or sphere-radius change rebuilds it. Resource balances, source movement, body movement, and camera state are not colliders and do not force a rebuild. A newly loaded runtime starts with no cache. The standalone `advance_life` API still builds a fresh scene per call; embedders can use `advance_life_cached` with a disposable cache. There is no incremental refit. The comparison itself remains linear in collider count and includes world validation; see [local reliability measurements](../research/world-reliability-experiment.md).

For a hit at fraction *t*, movement stops at `max(0, t − 1e−9/|v|)`, a `1e−9` world-unit backoff along nonzero motion. No sliding, friction, restitution, gravity, or detached fragments are implemented. Overlapping node spheres and capsules are independent solids. Their internal surfaces can stop the body early; this is not an exact regularized union or a watertight organism collider. Floating-point error outside the uncertainty heuristic can still produce a small penetration. The test suite covers high-speed sweeps, tangency, initial overlap, degenerate and parallel capsules, 1024 deterministic dense-scene sweeps, and 2048 sweeps through the 381-render-primitive scene (380 physical solids because the source marker is non-solid).

## Tick order and dynamic geometry

At a fixed tick, ordered events first change source state, topology, and desired velocity on a cloned world. Source sampling and organism growth follow, then ordinary sphere radii advance. Only then is the body swept against the resulting authoritative geometry. A growing solid may appear inside a stationary body: the body remains in place, receives `initial_overlap = true`, and cannot move while still overlapping. There is no silent teleport or automatic depenetration. Pruned colliders disappear before that tick's sweep. Any invalid event, unsupported numeric range, or indeterminate contact aborts the whole tick; `Runtime` also retains its pending events. The body and contact are drawn from the committed `WorldState` snapshot. CPU collision does not depend on `wgpu`.

Version-4 saves retain body state and pending velocity events. Versions 1–3 remain readable; absent bodies in those saves deserialize as `None`. Same-build continuation is checked by exact runtime equality. Cross-platform bitwise identity remains unproven. See [persistence](world-persistence.md), [replay](replay-determinism.md), and [local measurements](../research/first-contact-measurements.md).
